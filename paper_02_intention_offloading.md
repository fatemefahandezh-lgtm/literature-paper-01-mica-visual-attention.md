# Article 02: Outsourcing Memory to External Tools (Review)

- **Authors:** Sam J. Gilbert et al. (2023)
- **Journal:** Psychonomic Bulletin & Review
- **Paper Type:** Theoretical / Review Article
- **Focus Topic:** Intention Offloading & Metacognition

---

## 1. Core Summary
This paper reviews 5–10 years of research on *intention offloading*—how people decide whether to rely on internal memory or create external cues (e.g., smartphone alerts) to remember future actions.

## 2. Key Findings & Mechanisms
1. **Metacognition-Driven:** The decision to offload is guided by metacognitive confidence (beliefs about memory) rather than objective memory capacity alone.
2. **Effort Minimization:** People often over-use external tools (positive bias) to reduce cognitive effort and opportunity costs.
3. **Age & Lifespan:** 
   - Young children (<9 yrs) have metacognitive knowledge but lack strategic control to offload efficiently.
   - Older adults show reduced pro-reminder bias relative to performance needs and can suffer from overconfidence.

## 3. Operationalization & Tasks
- **Primary Paradigm:** Dragging numbered circles in sequence, with options to move delayed-target circles to edges as reminders.
- **Key Metrics:** Reminder-setting rate, task accuracy, and reminder bias (optimal vs. actual tool usage).

## 4. Connection to Project
Provides a framework for understanding when humans trust and delegate cognitive tasks to AI/external recommendation systems based on subjective confidence.
